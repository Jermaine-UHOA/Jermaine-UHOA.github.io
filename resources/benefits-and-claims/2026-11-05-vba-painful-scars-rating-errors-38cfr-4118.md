# VA OIG: 38% of painful scar disability ratings had errors — check your rating under 38 CFR § 4.118

*Published: November 5, 2026 · Category: Benefits & Claims · Source: VA Office of Inspector General (OIG)*

**At a glance**

- **Who:** Any veteran with a service-connected scar rated under **38 C.F.R. § 4.118, Diagnostic Codes 7800–7805**.
- **What:** A VA OIG review published October 21, 2026 found that **approximately 17,900 painful scar disability decisions (38% of a statistical sample) contained at least one rating error** between June 1, 2024 and May 31, 2025. A January 2026 follow-up sample showed a 43% error rate — the problem is not resolved.
- **Why it matters:** If your scar rating was processed during this window, there is a greater than 1-in-3 chance it contains an error. Many of these errors cost veterans retroactive benefits.

## What the OIG found

The Veterans Benefits Administration (VBA) is responsible for applying the VA Schedule for Rating Disabilities under **38 C.F.R. Part 4** when deciding disability claims. The OIG reviewed two statistical samples of painful scar decisions — one set that granted service connection, and one set that increased the disability rating for previously service-connected painful scars.

The three recurring deficiencies identified:

1. **Subjective pain reports accepted without objective examination findings.** Claims processors documented a veteran's subjective report of pain without the examiner's objective findings on physical examination — a shortcut that does not meet the evidentiary requirements under 38 C.F.R. § 4.118, Diagnostic Code 7804 (unstable or painful scars).

2. **Pyramiding — prohibited under 38 C.F.R. § 4.14.** VBA's claims-processing systems did not consistently flag "pyramiding," the improper practice of evaluating the same disability manifestation under multiple diagnostic codes. Pyramiding is expressly prohibited by **38 C.F.R. § 4.14** and inflates ratings while compounding inconsistencies across a veteran's claim.

3. **Missed separate evaluations under DC 7804.** When a previously service-connected scar later became painful, claims processors frequently failed to grant a **separate evaluation under Diagnostic Code 7804** — leaving veterans under-rated.

At least 920 of the errors identified resulted in approximately **$5.6 million in overpayments**, costs that will continue to accrue until VBA takes corrective action. VBA concurred with all three OIG recommendations.

## What to do if you have a service-connected scar

If you have a scar rated under **38 C.F.R. § 4.118 (Diagnostic Codes 7800, 7801, 7802, 7804, or 7805)**, pull your most recent VA Rating Decision and check:

- Did the C&P examination document **objective findings** of pain on examination (not just a reported history of pain)?
- Did the rater grant a **separate evaluation under DC 7804** for each painful scar, as 38 C.F.R. § 4.118 contemplates?
- Were any of your scars evaluated under **multiple diagnostic codes for the same manifestation** — a pyramiding violation under 38 C.F.R. § 4.14?
- If a previously service-connected scar later became painful and you reported it, was a separate evaluation under DC 7804 granted?

If any of these errors apply, you may file a **Supplemental Claim (VA Form 20-0995)** under 38 C.F.R. § 3.2501 citing the OIG findings as new and relevant evidence under the Appeals Modernization Act.
