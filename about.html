Our Story

About Unsung Heroes of America

A 501(c)(3) nonprofit born from the belief that every veteran who served deserves a stable, dignified life when they come home.

Our Mission

To house homeless veterans and provide the support they need to reclaim stable, independent lives.

Our Vision

To increase the number of shelters dedicated to recovery-oriented care for veterans in Georgia.

Founder's Story

Built by a Veteran Who Refused to Look Away

Unsung Heroes of America was founded by Jermaine Bullard Sr., Founder & Executive Director — a U.S. Army veteran from Fort Lauderdale, Florida, who enlisted in July 2001 and served for over a decade. He rose to the rank of Staff Sergeant (E-6), deployed three times to Iraq, and completed two tours in Kuwait — serving as a 19K tank armor crewman and 92W water treatment specialist, stationed in Germany and at Fort Stewart, Fort Riley, and Fort McPherson.

After leaving active duty in 2011, Mr. Bullard continued serving overseas as a Senior Training and Development Specialist, training the Afghan Army in supply chain logistics under U.S. Army regulations. Returning home, he saw firsthand how many of the men and women he had served alongside were falling through the cracks — living on the streets of a region with one of the highest concentrations of need in the country, often carrying the invisible wounds of war.

He founded UHOA in 2016 to answer that need with a small, intensely personal transitional shelter model. Mr. Bullard holds a Master of Science in Business Organizational Leadership and a Bachelor of Science in Business Administration from Columbia Southern University, and he personally seeded the organization's first investment. UHOA is currently in launch phase, with a 6–12 bed recovery home opening in Decatur, GA in 2027 (learn more).

The name says it all: these are unsung heroes — people who served without seeking recognition, and who too often struggle without receiving the help they've earned. UHOA exists to make sure those promises are kept.

Leadership

Who Runs UHOA

UHOA is a volunteer-led organization. Day-to-day operations, program design, and administration are led by our Founder & Executive Director, who serves without compensation. Every hour of leadership time and every dollar raised is directed toward standing up the transitional housing program and serving veterans.

As UHOA moves out of launch phase and into operating its first recovery home in 2027, we are actively expanding our leadership. We are building a Board of Directors composed of veterans, community leaders, housing and behavioral-health professionals, and grant-making partners who share our mission of ending veteran homelessness in metro Atlanta.

If you have professional experience in nonprofit governance, veteran services, real estate, behavioral health, finance, or fundraising and are interested in serving on our board, we would be honored to hear from you.

Our Approach

Why UHOA Looks Different

Large institutional shelters and short-stay emergency programs already exist. UHOA was built to fill a specific, persistent gap in the veteran homelessness continuum.

🏘️

Small & Personal

A 6–12 bed home instead of a 100-bed shelter. Small residence sizes make individualized case management, real accountability, and a genuine sense of community possible — which the research on veteran recovery consistently shows matters most.

🧭

Recovery-Oriented, Not Just Placement

We do not treat housing as the end of the journey. UHOA's model wraps stable housing around structured recovery support — mental health connections, VA benefits navigation, employment readiness, and life-skills development — so residents can leave prepared for independence.

🎖️

Veteran-Led

UHOA was founded and is led by a combat veteran who served three tours in Iraq. Every program decision is shaped by lived experience of military service, transition, and the specific challenges veterans face reintegrating into civilian life.

🧩

Filling the Gap

UHOA is designed to serve veterans who fall between VA-run programs and mainstream homeless shelters — including veterans returning from incarceration, veterans with substance-use histories, and veterans whose paperwork or discharge status creates barriers to traditional resources.

💵

Grassroots & Volunteer-Driven

UHOA operates with zero paid staff and minimal overhead. Every donation is directed toward launching and running the housing program, and every grant dollar is stewarded transparently — reported publicly through our Candid Gold Seal profile.

📍

Rooted in Metro Atlanta

We serve veterans in DeKalb, Fulton, Rockdale, and Gwinnett counties — a region with one of the highest concentrations of veterans experiencing homelessness in the Southeast. Local knowledge, local partnerships, and local accountability drive our work.

What We Stand For

Our Core Values

These principles guide everything we do — from the programs we run to the way we treat every veteran who walks through our door.

🤝

Dignity

Every veteran is treated with the respect and honor they deserve. We meet people where they are — without judgment, without barriers.

🎯

Individualized Care

No two veterans have the same story. We take time to understand each person's unique needs and build a support plan around them.

🔄

Whole-Person Recovery

We focus on recovery-oriented care — addressing housing, employment, mental health, and daily life skills together, not in isolation.

📢

Advocacy

We advocate loudly for veterans' rights to their earned benefits and push back against the systems that too often leave them behind.

🌱

Long-Term Success

We're not just meeting immediate needs — we're building pathways to lasting independence, employment, and community connection.

💡

Transparency

As a volunteer-driven 501(c)(3), we are committed to using every dollar and every hour of donated time where it matters most — our veterans.

Who We Serve

Veterans Who Need a Hand Up

We serve veterans of all branches, eras, and backgrounds who are experiencing or at risk of homelessness in the Atlanta metro area and surrounding communities. Our services are free for eligible veterans.

  • Veterans currently experiencing homelessness
  • Veterans at risk of losing stable housing
  • Veterans transitioning out of incarceration
  • Veterans struggling with substance use or mental health challenges
  • Veterans unaware of or unable to access VA benefits
  • Surviving spouses of veterans needing benefits guidance
